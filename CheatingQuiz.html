Cheating Checklist

Will Students Cheat on This Assignment?

Welcome! This self-assessment helps classroom instructors assess the likelihood that students will cheat in writing-intensive courses.

Hopefully, you'll leave with a few new strategies for making cheating less likely and learning more likely.

About

The advent of generative AI tools like ChatGPT has made cheating easier and less risky than ever before—and there's not much we can do about it. Detection tools are unreliable and increased surveillance undermines the trust and goodwill our classes depend on.

Thankfully, decades of research on cheating tells us that's the wrong approach, anyway. Instead of making cheating harder or riskier for students, we should be making cheating feel less attractive and less necessary.

How do we do that?

Thankfully, education research names specific features of classrooms and assignments that induce or reduce cheating behaviors. Most of these you've already heard of, and many are probably already visible in your class design.

There's more. The same features of assignments that reduce cheating also promote learning. In other words, cheating flourishes where learning flounders.

Wait. Are you saying it's my fault when students cheat?

Not quite! Cheating can happen even in the most thoughtfully designed classrooms, like yours. The point is, rather, that understanding cheaters shows us how to improve our classes for all students. And it's not by increasing surveillance or adding new hurdles, which might undermine the learning environment.

Instead, the answer is tweaking your syllabus and assignments in ways that address the root causes of cheating. Experts who study cheating suggest three environmental factors that can induce or reduce cheating. They tell us that humans are more likely to cheat when they feel:

  • low self-efficacy
  • low intrinsic motivation, and/or
  • high pressure to perform well

Therefore, we can discourage unsanctioned student use of generative AI tools by creating learning environments that promote self-efficacy, foster intrinsic motivation, and emphasize mastery over performance.

Drawing on popular learning guides as well as research on human learning, this checklist helps educators not only deter students who might cheat, but also improve the learning environment for everyone.
